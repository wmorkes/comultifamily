The Jackson Multifamily Market

Jackson Hole occupies a unique position in the Rocky Mountain multifamily landscape, defined by extreme supply constraint, world-class amenities, and a workforce housing shortage so acute that it has become a local political crisis. Teton County is consistently ranked among the most expensive counties in the United States, with median home prices that place ownership far out of reach for the hospitality, healthcare, and service workers who keep the resort economy functioning. The result is persistent, structural demand for rental housing at every price point.

Supply is constrained by a combination of forces that are essentially permanent: the surrounding Grand Teton National Park and Bridger-Teton National Forest control the vast majority of land in the valley, leaving a tiny developable footprint. The town of Jackson's land use regulations further restrict density, and construction costs at altitude with a short building season are among the highest in the intermountain West. Existing multifamily owners benefit from these barriers in ways that most markets cannot replicate.

The demand profile in Jackson is bifurcated. Ultra-high-income seasonal and permanent residents compete for a small stock of luxury rentals, while the workforce housing segment serves the thousands of employees in hospitality, ski operations, healthcare, and retail who cannot afford ownership. Both segments face vacancy rates that are among the lowest we see in any market we cover. Wyoming's zero income tax is an additional draw for high-income residents seeking to establish state residency.

Jackson Hole Communities We Cover

The town of Jackson is the most active multifamily location in the valley, with proximity to employment, retail, and services that makes it the first choice for workforce renters. Properties in central Jackson command the strongest rents and lowest vacancy in the market and trade infrequently given the depth of owner conviction in long-term hold value. When assets do come to market here, they attract competitive interest from buyers who recognize the irreplaceable nature of the location.

Teton Village, at the base of Jackson Hole Mountain Resort, serves a highly seasonal workforce in ski operations, hospitality, and resort services. Workforce housing at the Village trades differently from central Jackson, with higher seasonality but also elevated peak-season demand that can generate strong revenue during the winter months. Buyers considering Teton Village assets need to underwrite the seasonal pattern carefully and evaluate year-round occupancy assumptions against the resort's employment data.

Wilson and the South Park area of Teton County offer a more rural character with smaller-scale multifamily and single-family rental product. Victor and Driggs, Idaho, across Teton Pass, have increasingly absorbed Jackson Hole workforce housing demand as Jackson rents have pushed essential workers further from the valley floor. We track buyer interest in both sides of the pass and can discuss cross-state considerations with buyers and sellers evaluating the full Jackson Hole regional housing market.

Common Questions

Jackson Multifamily — Frequently Asked Questions

What makes Jackson, Wyoming multifamily unique?
Jackson Hole is among the most supply-constrained resort communities in the nation. Grand Teton National Park and Bridger-Teton National Forest effectively encircle the valley, making new development extraordinarily difficult. The result is acute workforce housing scarcity, consistently strong occupancy, and rent levels that rival major urban markets.
Who buys apartment buildings in Jackson, Wyoming?
Jackson multifamily assets attract ultra-high-net-worth individuals, family offices, and resort-market specialists. No-state-income-tax Wyoming ownership combined with Jackson's exceptional lifestyle profile creates a compelling long-term hold case for investors who prioritize capital preservation.
Is Jackson multifamily affected by short-term rental competition?
Short-term rental activity has significantly reduced available long-term rental stock in Jackson, which paradoxically supports occupancy and rents for traditional apartment operators. The workforce housing shortage is severe enough that there is sustained demand from hospitality, healthcare, and service sector employees year-round.
What is the workforce housing shortage like in Jackson Hole?
Jackson Hole's workforce housing shortage is severe and well-documented. A significant portion of the employees who work in Jackson's hospitality, healthcare, and service sectors cannot afford to live in the valley and commute long distances or rely on employer-provided housing. Teton County has implemented workforce housing programs and inclusionary requirements for new development, but supply additions have not kept pace with demand. This structural imbalance creates sustained occupancy for existing multifamily owners and represents a genuine long-term tailwind for the asset class.
How does Teton County's land use policy affect multifamily values?
Teton County maintains strict land use regulations designed to preserve the valley's character and manage growth. The combination of federal land surrounding the valley and local zoning restrictions makes meaningful new multifamily development extremely difficult and expensive. For existing owners, this supply barrier is the single most important factor supporting long-term value, as it ensures that new competitive product cannot enter the market at scale regardless of demand conditions.
What cap rates do Jackson Hole apartments actually trade at?
Jackson Hole multifamily cap rates are among the lowest in Wyoming and the broader Rocky Mountain region, typically ranging from the high 3s to the mid-5s depending on asset quality, location, and tenant profile. Wyoming's non-disclosure status means public data is limited, but cap rates here reflect the supply-constraint premium, strong appreciation expectations, and the market's position as a trophy real estate destination. Sellers should calibrate expectations to national resort market comparables rather than other Wyoming cities.
How long does it take to sell a Jackson Hole multifamily asset?
Transaction timelines in Jackson can be longer than Front Range markets, reflecting a smaller pool of qualified buyers and the complexity of Wyoming non-disclosure state transactions where financing may require creative structuring. A well-positioned asset at an appropriate price typically generates serious interest within 30 to 60 days of confidential or public marketing, but closing timelines of 60 to 90 days are common. For the right asset, patient and targeted marketing to national capital sources consistently produces better outcomes than a quick local process.
